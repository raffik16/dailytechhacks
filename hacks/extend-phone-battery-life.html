How to Extend Phone Battery Life: 10 Proven Hacks for 2025

📅 Updated: January 2025 ⏱️ 8 min read 📱 iPhone & Android

Is your phone battery draining faster than ever? You're not alone. With larger screens, more powerful processors, and apps constantly running in the background, modern smartphones consume more power than ever before. The good news is that you can extend your phone battery life by 30-50% with a few simple changes to your settings and habits.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll walk you through 10 proven battery-saving hacks that work on both iPhone and Android devices. These aren't just theoretical tips—they're tested solutions that can make a real difference in how long your phone lasts between charges. Whether you're dealing with a phone that dies by lunchtime or just want to squeeze more life out of your battery during travel, these hacks will help.

Why Your Phone Battery Drains So Fast

Before diving into solutions, it's helpful to understand what's eating your battery. The biggest culprits are typically:

  • Screen brightness: Your display consumes 30-50% of total battery power
  • Background apps: Apps refreshing data even when you're not using them
  • Location services: GPS constantly tracking your position
  • Push notifications: Waking your phone hundreds of times daily
  • Weak cellular signal: Your phone works harder to maintain connection

Now let's tackle each of these with actionable solutions.

10 Proven Hacks to Extend Phone Battery Life

  1. Enable Low Power Mode (Saves 20-30%)

    This is the single most effective way to extend battery life instantly. Low Power Mode reduces background activity, auto-downloads, and visual effects.

    On iPhone: Go to Settings → Battery → Low Power Mode, or ask Siri to turn it on.

    On Android: Go to Settings → Battery → Battery Saver (varies by manufacturer).

    Pro tip: You can automate this to turn on at 30% or 20% battery remaining.

  2. Reduce Screen Brightness (Saves 15-25%)

    Your screen is the biggest battery hog. Reducing brightness from 100% to 50% can nearly double your screen-on time.

    Better solution: Enable auto-brightness so your phone adjusts based on ambient light. This provides good visibility while conserving power.

    On iPhone: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Auto-Brightness

    On Android: Settings → Display → Adaptive brightness

  3. Disable Background App Refresh (Saves 10-15%)

    Many apps refresh their content in the background even when you're not using them. Facebook, Instagram, email apps—they're all guilty.

    On iPhone: Settings → General → Background App Refresh. Turn it off entirely or select specific apps.

    On Android: Settings → Apps → [App Name] → Battery → Restrict background activity

  4. Manage Location Services Wisely (Saves 10-20%)

    GPS is incredibly power-hungry. While you probably need it for maps and ride-sharing, most apps don't need your location constantly.

    Best practice: Set apps to "While Using" instead of "Always" for location access. Review which apps have location permission and revoke access from apps that don't need it.

    On iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services

    On Android: Settings → Location → App location permissions

  5. Turn Off Push Email (Saves 5-10%)

    Push email keeps a constant connection to your mail server, draining battery. Switch to fetching email manually or at intervals.

    On iPhone: Settings → Mail → Accounts → Fetch New Data. Set to "Hourly" or "Manually" instead of Push.

    On Android: Open your email app settings and change sync frequency to every 30 minutes or hourly.

  6. Use Dark Mode on OLED Screens (Saves 10-20%)

    If your phone has an OLED or AMOLED display (most flagship phones do), dark mode can significantly save battery because black pixels are actually turned off.

    On iPhone: Settings → Display & Brightness → Dark

    On Android: Settings → Display → Dark theme

    Bonus: Many apps have their own dark mode setting too—enable it in frequently used apps.

  7. Disable Unnecessary Notifications

    Every notification wakes your screen and processor. Reduce notifications to only essential apps.

    On iPhone: Settings → Notifications. Go through each app and disable notifications for non-essential ones.

    On Android: Settings → Apps & notifications → Notifications

  8. Turn Off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth When Not Needed

    While modern phones are efficient, these radios still consume power scanning for connections. Turn them off when you don't need them, especially Bluetooth if you're not using wireless accessories.

    Quick tip: Use airplane mode in areas with poor cellular coverage—your phone works overtime trying to find signal.

  9. Reduce Screen Timeout Duration

    Set your screen to turn off after 30 seconds of inactivity instead of 2 minutes. This small change adds up throughout the day.

    On iPhone: Settings → Display & Brightness → Auto-Lock

    On Android: Settings → Display → Screen timeout

  10. Check Battery Usage and Kill Draining Apps

    Both iOS and Android show you exactly which apps consume the most battery. Check this regularly and address problem apps.

    On iPhone: Settings → Battery (scroll down for app usage)

    On Android: Settings → Battery → Battery usage

    If an app you rarely use is consuming significant battery, consider uninstalling it or restricting its background activity.

💡 Pro Tip: Battery Health Matters

If your phone is over 2 years old and battery life has decreased significantly, check your battery health. On iPhone, go to Settings → Battery → Battery Health. If it's below 80%, consider a battery replacement—it's much cheaper than a new phone and can restore original battery life.

Bonus Tips for Maximum Battery Life

  • Avoid extreme temperatures: Heat degrades battery capacity permanently. Don't leave your phone in a hot car.
  • Use original or certified chargers: Cheap chargers can damage battery health over time.
  • Don't charge to 100% daily: Keeping your battery between 20-80% extends its long-term lifespan.
  • Update your OS: Software updates often include battery optimizations.

Conclusion: Small Changes, Big Impact

You don't need to implement all 10 hacks to see results. Start with the top three—Low Power Mode, reduced brightness, and managing background app refresh—and you'll likely see a 30-40% improvement in battery life immediately.

The key is finding the right balance between battery life and functionality. Some people are fine with aggressive settings, while others prefer convenience over battery life. Experiment with these hacks to find what works best for your usage patterns.
